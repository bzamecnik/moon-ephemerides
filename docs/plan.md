# Plan

## Goal

An interactive page showing how the Moon's phases (synodic month) and its
distance (anomalistic month) beat against each other: the events around any
date, the long-term pattern, and the moments where the two rhythms line up
best (Δ between syzygy and apsis ≈ 0, symmetric neighbours), plus nodes and
eclipses as the third rhythm. Valuable on its own; a possible later use is a
careful statistical test of the earthquake hypothesis (see
`../moon-earthquake-experiments/`).

## Status

Released 0.1.0 (see `CHANGELOG.md`). M1 done: the Python catalog reproduces the astropixels supermoon/micromoon
tables for 1901–2100 (same events, times to the minute, Δ to 0.008 d), see
`validation.md`. M3 done. M4: the table, the beat chart, the phase-vs-distance
chart (linear interpolation between events) and the ranking of the best
alignments are in, as tabs sharing the selected moment, plus a distance-vs-time
chart with the Moon's height above the ecliptic, the orbit seen from above in
the frame of the Sun, a long-term view (one event per half beat over decades)
and keyboard navigation by event and by beat. Solar and lunar eclipses are in
the catalog and the views; `docs/astronomy.md` is shown as a learning page.
Positions between events come from astronomy-engine. Next: M5 envelopes.

## M5 – Envelopes and scores

**Envelopes.** The extremes are not constant: perigee distance ranges over
~356 400–370 400 km, apogee over ~404 000–406 700 km, modulated mainly by the
angle between the Sun and the line of apsides and by the Earth–Sun distance.
An envelope (the curve through the extremes) gives limits, so that any event
can be placed between "as close as it gets at this time" and "as far": a
normalised score. Two ways:

1. empirical: the upper/lower hull of the apsis distances over time
   (local extrema of the extrema, smoothed or interpolated);
2. physical: fit the apsis distances to a few periodic terms (twice the
   Sun–apse angle, the Sun's anomaly), as in the perigee/apogee series of
   Meeus (Astronomical Algorithms, ch. 50); this explains the envelope instead
   of just tracing it.

The same works for Δ: the best Δ of each 411.8-day season varies, and its
envelope shows when the best alignments of a whole era happen.
